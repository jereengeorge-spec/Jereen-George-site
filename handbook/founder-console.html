TechButler is a subscription that becomes an aging parent's tech support, sold to their adult child so that child stops being the family's unpaid IT department. This console is the operating layer: what is live, what is blocked, how money is made, and what to do each day. Verified against the codebase 9 August 2026.

The business model

An AI companion named George handles day-to-day support; a human handles escalations. The adult child owns the account, the senior is a managed profile.

The buyer pays

Adult child, 35–65, household income $150k+, living 30+ minutes from the parent. Distance is the qualifier — proximity is what makes people say "I'll just drive over." Buys not getting the call.

The user uses

Their parent, 70+. Wants independence and someone patient who never makes them feel stupid. Never sees a bill.

Untapped second buyer open

Affluent 65–75-year-olds buying for a spouse or themselves. Online, moneyed, embarrassed to keep asking their kids — and nobody is selling to them. Same product, dignity script instead of relief script.

The positioning rule that cannot be broken: never market this as "unlimited tech support for seniors." Candoo sells exactly that, human-delivered, for $228/yr against our $1,188/yr. On that framing we lose on price and lose again on "a human beats an AI."

The premium rests on four things competitors structurally cannot do: 24/7 zero-wait (they are appointment-based), the buyer's outcome rather than the user's, the family dashboard, and proactive scam prevention — insurance framing carries insurance pricing.

Channel plan

Because the target is ~150 customers rather than an audience, the ranking is not the usual one: relationships beat reach. Phases 1–3 run concurrently; phase 4 is gated on phase 0 shipping.

00

Money model now

Annual-first as the default, a "cut the calls in half or refund" guarantee, the billing-copy fix, and the scam-proof checklist lead magnet. Produces an offer that advertising could survive.

01

Warm outreach weeks 1–2

100 conversations. Free and highest-trust — nearly everyone you know aged 35–65 has aging parents. The goal is not revenue: it is 5–10 real users and the testimonials every other channel depends on.

02

Referral partnerships weeks 2–8

Elder-law attorneys, wealth managers, concierge medicine, geriatric care managers, private-pay home care, senior communities, CPAs. Each sits on a room of income-qualified buyers, lends their credibility, and has a live pain we fix. The fastest route to 150 — and the phase with no CRM support today.

03

Faceless George content weeks 1–12, parallel

The compounding asset. Takes 60–90 days to produce real inbound, so start immediately and judge late. Because George is also the in-product AI, every video doubles as a product demo — there is no bridge to cross from audience to offer.

04

Paid ads month 3+

The best-performing organic videos with a CTA attached, targeted by income proxy. Opens only if phase 0 shipped; without annual-first the CAC ceiling of $37 keeps this closed permanently.

Daily dose, phases 1–3: 100 outreach touches or 100 minutes of content, every weekday. Nothing else on this page matters if that does not happen.

The CRM

There is no external CRM — no HubSpot, Salesforce, Pipedrive, Airtable, or Notion anywhere in the codebase. What exists is a home-built founder console at /founder backed directly by Supabase Postgres. Good for this size: no seat cost, no sync, no export lock-in. The cost is that it only does what someone wrote.

Console panels

/founder

MRR, active / trialing / canceled, conversion rate, six-month signup and MRR trend, open escalations, average resolution hours, oldest open ticket, seniors, leads, feedback, and quick links into Supabase, Stripe and Vercel.

/founder/tickets

The work queue plus a detail view. Two actions: set status open or resolved (stamping the resolution time), and add a founder-private note.

The gap that matters: the CRM tracks delivery, not acquisition. Every table above describes someone who already signed up, plus one inbound form. But the business runs on warm outreach and referral partnerships producing 3–4 customers a week, and there is nowhere to record any of it.

The leads table is name, email, phone, problem, timestamp — and nothing else. No status. No source, so a form fill and an attorney's referral look identical. No next action, so nothing surfaces "you said you'd follow up Tuesday." No partner concept at all. No outreach log, so the Rule of 100 cannot be counted. This is the top-priority CRM build — a written, additive migration adding partners, lead_notes, touches and pipeline fields is ready to run in handbook/CRM.md.

Operating rhythms

The week runs on three loops. The daily one is roughly 90 minutes.

Daily ~90 min

  • Escalation sweep, morning and evening — nothing comes first, response time is the product
  • Scam alerts on notification, urgent at any hour
  • Rule of 100 — 100 touches or 100 minutes of content
  • Pipeline due list — anything past its next-action date
  • Comment replies within 2 hours of posting

Weekly ~60 min

  • Record the seven metrics — same day, same time, always
  • Pipeline review: every lead gets a next action or a lost reason
  • Partner review: every active partner gets a touch
  • Batch next week's videos in one sitting
  • Export Supabase — this is the entire disaster-recovery plan

Monthly ~2 hrs

  • Cohort check — trial-to-paid conversion, churn by signup month
  • Channel attribution — MRR by lead source; the decision the whole plan waits on
  • COGS reality check against the $25 assumption
  • Stripe reconciliation — failed deposits, involuntary churn, disputes
  • Update this handbook the same day anything changes

Standard procedures

The five things that happen over and over. Each has one correct sequence.

Human escalation SOP-1

  1. Alert arrives by email via Resend
  2. Read the AI transcript first — the customer already explained it once and must never repeat it
  3. Contact them directly. Target: under 2 hours in waking hours
  4. Log it as a founder-private ticket note
  5. Mark resolved — this feeds the resolution-time metric

Scam alert SOP-2

  1. Fires from George detecting a pattern, or the senior pressing their own SOS button
  2. System emails the adult child, not the founder, and banners their dashboard
  3. Confirm the owner responded; if nothing within an hour, contact them yourself
  4. Mark handled

The single most brand-defining moment in the product — it is what the subscription is really bought for.

New customer SOP-3

  1. Checkout opens at signup. No access until the webhook confirms — an abandoned checkout must never yield free access
  2. Webhook sets trialing, charges the $25 deposit, attaches the day-8 coupon
  3. Confirm deposit status is paid; if failed, see RUN-4
  4. Personal welcome within 24 hours — the cheapest retention there is
  5. Confirm they added a senior. An owner with zero seniors will churn

Working a partner SOP-4

  1. Add to partners with a category and a next-action date
  2. Lead with their problem: clients keep asking them for tech help they don't want to give
  3. Give first — hand them the scam-proof checklist to pass on under their own name
  4. Log every contact
  5. On activation, agree how referrals arrive and tag them, or attribution is lost

Producing a video SOP-5

  1. Take the next scriptbook entry
  2. Run it through the local pipeline and check the render
  3. Post, then reply to comments for two hours
  4. Log the numbers

Locked: faceless · mascot is George, the same name as the in-product AI · the founder appears as a name and a voice, never a face.

Runbooks

For when something breaks, or is about to.

RUN-2 · Stripe test → live

Six values must change together. Swapping only the two keys is the standard failure — products, prices, coupons and webhook endpoints are separate objects in test and live, and none of those IDs carry over.

VariableLive value
STRIPE_SECRET_KEYsk_live_…
NEXT_PUBLIC_STRIPE_PUBLISHABLE_KEYpk_live_…
STRIPE_PRICE_MAINnew live-mode price ID ($99/mo)
STRIPE_PRICE_ANNUALnew live-mode price ID ($999/yr)
STRIPE_COUPON_FIRST_MONTHnew live-mode coupon ID ($25 off, once)
STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRETwhsec_… from a new live endpoint

Verify in 60 seconds: start a signup on the live site and look at Stripe Checkout. Test mode paints an orange TEST MODE banner. No banner means live — and unlike reading dashboard settings, this proves the deployed app is live. Separately confirm the Stripe account itself is activated with business details and a bank account; without activation, live keys do not exist at all.

Three silent failure modes, all verified in the code. A missing secret key falls back to a placeholder and the checkout button simply does nothing — no crash. A missing live annual price makes the annual option vanish from the UI and every checkout fall back to monthly, with no error. And a stale test-mode coupon ID fails inside a non-fatal catch, so the customer is charged $99 on day 8 instead of $74 — a silent overcharge visible only in server logs.

Webhook not firing RUN-3

Symptoms: signups stuck without a subscription status, no deposits, no coupons. Check the live-mode endpoint exists, that all four events are subscribed, and that recent deliveries return 200. Signature failures mean the webhook secret does not match that endpoint. Stripe retries, so fixing the secret recovers pending events.

Deposit failed RUN-4

Usually a card requiring 3D Secure, which cannot complete off-session. The billing page prompts the customer to update their payment method. Deposits are idempotency-keyed on the subscription ID, so a webhook retry returns the original charge and never double-bills.

Data loss RUN-5

Supabase holds the only copy of every customer record. The weekly export is the disaster-recovery plan. Restore means: new project, run the schema, run the migrations, import the CSVs. Store exports somewhere that is not Supabase.

Founder access is a single environment variable. FOUNDER_EMAILS is the entire permission model for the console. Add an email and redeploy to grant; remove and redeploy to revoke.

There are no roles and no audit log. Anyone on that list sees all revenue, every customer, every support transcript, and every senior's details. Grant it deliberately.

Handoff checklist

  • Add the successor to FOUNDER_EMAILS, redeploy, confirm the console loads
  • Transfer or share Vercel, Supabase, Stripe, Anthropic, Resend, registrar and GitHub
  • Move every credential into a shared password-manager vault
  • Walk SOP-1 and SOP-2 together on a real ticket — response time is the product
  • Hand over this console and confirm they can reach it
  • Provide a fresh Supabase export
  • Confirm they can run the video pipeline end to end
  • Introduce them to every active partner personally — partnerships are relationships and do not transfer through a database row
  • Remove the departing person from FOUNDER_EMAILS, redeploy, and rotate every shared secret
