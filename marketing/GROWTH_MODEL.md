# Growth Model — which path, and why

Companion to [`PLAYBOOK.md`](PLAYBOOK.md). That doc answers *how to make content*.
This one answers *which channel actually gets to income replacement*, using Hormozi's
frameworks applied to the real numbers.

> Part of the [handbook](../handbook/README.md) — see
> [`BUSINESS_MODEL.md`](../handbook/BUSINESS_MODEL.md) for the model this strategy serves,
> [`OPERATIONS.md`](../handbook/OPERATIONS.md) for the rhythms that execute it, and
> [`CRM.md`](../handbook/CRM.md) for where the pipeline gets tracked.

**The goal, stated plainly:** replace your income with TechButler subscription revenue.
Faceless AI brand as the promotion engine. Price stays at $99/mo — the audience is
income-qualified rather than price-sensitive.

---

## 1. The number that reframes everything

You are not building an audience business. You are looking for a specific, small
number of affluent families.

Assume COGS ≈ **$25/customer/month** (Anthropic API + your share of human escalation).

| Plan | Revenue/customer/mo | Gross profit/customer/mo |
|---|---|---|
| $99/mo | $99 | **$74** |
| $999/yr | $83.25 | **$58** |

**Active customers needed to replace income:**

| Target income | Monthly GP needed | On $99/mo | On $999/yr |
|---|---|---|---|
| $60,000 | $5,000 | 68 | 86 |
| $100,000 | $8,333 | 113 | 143 |
| $150,000 | $12,500 | 169 | 215 |
| $200,000 | $16,667 | 225 | 286 |

*(Gross profit, before taxes and other operating costs — treat it as the floor, not the target.)*

**So: roughly 70–225 paying customers is the whole game.** Not a million views. Not
a hundred thousand followers. Somewhere around **150 families**.

At ~6%/month churn, holding 113 customers means replacing ~7/month. Getting from 0 to
113 inside a year means landing **3–4 new customers per week**. That is the actual job.

This number is why the channel recommendation below is what it is. A strategy that
needs virality to produce 150 customers is the wrong strategy when 150 customers can be
found one conversation at a time.

## 2. The pricing reality (context, not an objection)

Named competitors in remote senior tech support:

| Service | Price | Model |
|---|---|---|
| HelloTech (remote-only) | **$99/year** ($9.99/mo) | Human, on-demand |
| Best Buy Total | **$179.99/year** | Human, plus retail perks |
| Candoo Tech | **$228/yr** single, **$340/yr** couple | Human, senior-specialist advisors |
| Candoo one-off session | $75/session | Human |
| **TechButler** | **$1,188/year** ($99/mo) | AI 24/7 + human escalation + family dashboard |

TechButler is **3.5×–12× the price of every named competitor.** That is a fact about
the market, and holding the price is a legitimate call — but it forces one thing:

> **You cannot market TechButler as "unlimited tech support for seniors."**
> Candoo sells that, human-delivered, for $228/year. On that framing you lose on price
> and lose again on "but a human is better than an AI."

The premium has to come from what the cheap options structurally cannot do:

- **24/7, zero-wait.** Candoo and HelloTech are appointment-based. George answers at 11pm on a Sunday.
- **The buyer's outcome, not the user's.** Competitors sell help *to the senior*. TechButler sells **"you stop getting the call"** *to the adult child*. Different product, different buyer, different price ceiling.
- **The family dashboard.** Nobody at $228/yr shows you how your mom is actually doing. That's peace-of-mind, and peace-of-mind is where premium pricing lives.
- **Proactive scam protection.** Not "we fix it when it breaks" — "we stopped it before it happened." Insurance framing, insurance pricing.

$99/mo is defensible against $228/yr. "Unlimited tech help" at $99/mo is not.
Same price, different sentence.

## 3. Which Hormozi path — the answer

Hormozi's **Core Four** is every advertising method that exists, sorted two ways:
warm/cold × one-to-one/one-to-many.

| | One-to-one | One-to-many |
|---|---|---|
| **Warm** (they know you) | Warm outreach | Free content |
| **Cold** (strangers) | Cold outreach | Paid ads |

His prescribed order when you have no money and no audience: **warm outreach → content
→ cold outreach → paid ads.** Start where trust is highest and cost is zero; only touch
paid ads once the offer is proven.

His **Rule of 100** sets the daily dose: 100 outreaches, *or* 100 minutes of content,
*or* $100 of ad spend. Every day. That's the price of admission on any path.

### Scored for your situation

**1. Warm outreach — START HERE. Week 1.**
Free, highest trust, fastest to first dollar. Your network is unusually well-matched:
essentially everyone you know aged 35–65 has aging parents. Hormozi's script is not
"do you want this" — it's *acknowledge, compliment specifically, then ask who they know
who fits the description*. That third move is what makes it not feel like selling.
The goal for the first 100 conversations is not revenue. It's **5–10 real users and
the testimonials that make every other channel work.**

**2. Cold outreach (partnerships) — THE SLEEPER. Weeks 2–8. Most underrated path here.**
This is where 150 customers comes from fastest, and it is the path your current plan
completely ignores. One relationship = many customers, at near-zero CAC:

- Elder-law attorneys and estate planners (affluent clients, by definition)
- Financial advisors and wealth managers with retiree books
- Concierge/private medicine practices
- Geriatric care managers and private-pay home care agencies
- Independent-living and CCRC communities (private-pay, not Medicaid)
- CPAs with high-net-worth retiree clients

Every one of these already has a roomful of exactly your buyer — **income-qualified,
already paying for professional services, already worried about their parent.** They
also solve your hardest problem for free: these professionals are *trusted*, so they
carry your credibility. And they have a live pain you fix — their clients keep asking
them for tech help they don't want to give.

**3. Free content (faceless George) — THE DURABLE ASSET. Start day 1, judge at day 90.**
Runs in parallel from the beginning, but be honest about the timeline: organic short-form
takes 60–90 days to produce meaningful inbound. Its real payoff is threefold — it
compounds while you sleep, it warms up partnership and outreach conversations before
you have them, and **it becomes your ad creative** in phase 4. Hormozi's own observation
is that the gap between organic and paid has closed: his ads are just organic videos with
a CTA button.

**4. Paid ads — BLOCKED, and here's the exact math that unblocks it.**

Hormozi's client-financed acquisition rule: **30-day gross profit > 2 × CAC.**

*On the current monthly offer:* $25 today + $74 on day 8 = **$99 in 30 days.** Minus
$25 COGS = $74 GP. Max allowable CAC = **$37.**
A $37 CAC for a $99/mo consumer subscription in the caregiving space is not achievable
on any platform. **Paid ads are mathematically closed to you today.**

*On an annual-first offer:* $999 collected upfront = **$999 in 30 days.** Minus ~$25
first-month COGS = ~$974 GP. Max allowable CAC = **~$487.**
A $487 CAC for a high-intent, high-income caregiving offer is very achievable.

**That single change — making annual the default instead of the alternative — moves
paid ads from impossible to profitable, without touching the price.** Same $999, just
collected at the front instead of the middle. It also near-eliminates monthly churn,
which is the other thing quietly killing the model.

## 4. Your instinct, corrected

> *"Build the brand first, then point people to the software."*

Directionally right, structurally wrong — and your own two locked decisions already
resolved it.

**First:** faceless + personal brand is a contradiction. A personal brand with no face
and no name isn't a personal brand. By choosing faceless and choosing George, you have
already picked the business/mascot brand. The question is answered.

**Second, and this is the part worth internalizing:** George is not a mascot *for* the
product. Per the design brief, **George is the product** — the voice-first AI companion
seniors actually talk to. That collapses the usual gap. Most faceless channels have to
build an audience and *then* awkwardly bridge to "now buy my thing." You don't have that
bridge to cross. **Every George video is simultaneously brand-building and a product
demo.** Someone who watches five George videos has met the product five times.

So "brand first, monetize later" is a false choice in your case. They're the same motion.

**Third:** Hormozi's actual position on this is the opposite of the popular version.
*"If you're really into building a personal brand, start with ads and an offer first —
if you can get strangers to buy from you without a personal brand, it will only work
better WITH a personal brand."* Prove the offer converts first; brand is an amplifier,
not a substitute. He also warns that a business scaled on a personal brand is inherently
weak and hard to exit — which is a second argument for the George/mascot route. A mascot
brand is a transferable asset. Your face is not.

**Where you should appear:** as a name and a voice, never a face. "I'm Jereen, I built
this because I was my family's IT department" — in the bio, in captions, in every comment
reply. That buys the human-trust premium (personal brands do out-engage business brands
2–3×) without breaking the faceless constraint or making the business dependent on you.

**And the faceless choice is quietly the right production call anyway:** faceless creators
ship 3–5× more often at the same quality, and over a year that volume advantage outweighs
the per-video engagement edge that on-camera creators enjoy. Sabrina's own law —
*25 videos in 32 days beats 11 in 100* — says the same thing.

## 5. Audience: broaden the age, narrow the income

You asked to widen the generation. Correct instinct, but the axis that matters is income,
not age.

**Primary buyer:** adult children **35–65** (widened from 40–55), household income
**$150k+**, professional/managerial, living 30+ minutes from the parent. The distance
matters more than the age — proximity is what makes people say "I'll just drive over."

**Second buyer, currently unaddressed:** affluent **65–75-year-olds buying for a spouse
or for themselves.** They are online, they have money, they are embarrassed to keep
asking their kids, and *nobody is selling to them.* Same product, entirely different
script — dignity and independence instead of relief and guilt.

**Targeting proxies for income-qualification** (the ads layer, later): homeowners in
high-value ZIPs, job titles in the professional bracket, interests around wealth
management/private schools/travel, and lookalikes built from your first partnership
customers — who will be affluent by construction.

**What this means for content:** the scriptbook's emotional pillars (A: scam, D: identity)
still lead — those are universal. But the CTA and the product framing must read premium.
"$99 = peace of mind" (S28) is the right idea aimed slightly low; against a $150k+
household the frame is *"less than one hour of your time per month."*

## 6. Money model fixes, ranked by leverage

Hormozi's attraction-offer types applied to what you already have:

**1. Make annual the default. (Highest leverage by an order of magnitude.)**
$999 upfront takes 30-day cash from $99 → $999. Unlocks paid ads, kills monthly churn,
finances the next customer. Present monthly as the *downsell*, not the headline.
His own mechanism for this: waive a fee in exchange for the annual commitment.

**2. Add a "win your money back" guarantee.**
*"If George doesn't cut your parent's tech calls to you by half in 60 days, full refund."*
This niche is perfect for it — the promise is measurable, the buyer already counts those
calls, and it directly de-risks a price that is 4× the alternatives.

**3. Formalize `/stuck` as the decoy.**
You already built the free tier. Hormozi's decoy pattern — stripped-down free next to
premium — hit an 80% take rate on premium in his gyms. Right now `/stuck` is a lead
capture; make it explicitly the free tier so the premium has something to be measured
against.

**4. Fix the billing copy before any [Trial] CTA runs.** *(Carried from PLAYBOOK.)*
Scriptbook says "first week's basically free." Billing charges **$25 today**. Say
"$25 today, cancel any time in the first week." The gap between ad copy and first
card charge is how you collect chargebacks and platform strikes.

**5. Build the lead magnet.**
Hormozi: *give away the secrets, sell the implementation* — the free thing should be
good enough that people think "if this is free, the paid thing must be incredible."
Yours writes itself: **"The Scam-Proof Parent Checklist"** — the 3 settings, the family
safe word, the gift-card rule. It's already inside S4/S6/S7. Gate it for an email, use
it as the [Help] destination, and it becomes the asset partners hand to their clients.

## 7. The plan

| Phase | Weeks | Channel | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0 — Money model** | Now | Annual-first, guarantee, copy fix, lead magnet | Offer that ads could survive |
| **1 — Warm** | 1–2 | 100 conversations, Hormozi's 3-step script | 5–10 users + testimonials |
| **2 — Partnerships** | 2–8 | 20 professional referral partners | 3–4 customers/week |
| **3 — Content** | 1–12 (parallel) | Faceless George Shorts, daily | Compounding inbound + ad creative |
| **4 — Paid** | 12+ | Best organic videos + CTA, income-targeted | Scale to 150 customers |

Phases 1–3 start together. Phase 4 only opens if phase 0 shipped — the annual-first
change is the gate, and without it phase 4 stays closed permanently.

**Daily dose (Rule of 100), phases 1–3:** 100 outreach touches *or* 100 minutes of
content. Every weekday. Nothing else on this page matters if that doesn't happen.

**Track weekly:** conversations had · partners signed · videos posted · leads
captured · trials started · **paying customers** · churn.
Only the last two are real. The rest are leading indicators.
